# Website-value flyer: verified research and investment example

The original five statistics were restored in website-value v4 and retained in v5, with source dates and corrected wording. The bracketed references printed below the figures link directly to these primary sources in the PDF.

## [1] BrightLocal: local search and decision speed, 2026

[Consumer Search Behavior: Speed, June 24, 2026](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/consumer-search-behavior-speed/)

Supports 84% reporting an online local-business search during the prior three months, 75% taking less than 30 minutes to choose, and 28% taking less than five minutes. The study describes a US consumer panel; these are survey findings, not a guarantee about any individual business. BrightLocal reports 1,227 recent local searchers in its methodology but does not disclose a separate screening denominator for the 84% figure. The flyer therefore attributes 84% to BrightLocal rather than calling it 84% of those 1,227 respondents.

## [2] BrightLocal: mobile search, 2026

[Consumer Search Behavior: Channels, July 9, 2026](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/consumer-search-behavior-channels/)

Supports 73% beginning their most recent local-business search on a mobile phone. This is a respondent-level recollection of the latest search, not a measured share of every local search. The flyer keeps that distinction.

## [3] Stanford / Consumer WebWatch: credibility, 2002

[How Do People Evaluate a Web Site's Credibility?](https://credibility.stanford.edu/pdf/How_Do_People_Evaluate_a_Web_Site%27s_Credibility_v37.pdf)

Released October 29, 2002; updated November 11, 2002. The abstract reports that 46.1% of credibility comments mentioned visual design. The flyer uses that precise unit rather than rounding to 46% of all consumers. The study's date is printed directly under the figure; it is historical evidence, not a new survey.

## [4] Google: mobile load time, 2016 data

[Google: Introducing the mobile Speed Scorecard and Impact Calculator](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/ads/speed-scorecard-impact-calculator/)

This official February 26, 2018 article attributes the 53% mobile-visit abandonment figure to Google's 2016 data. The underlying benchmark used aggregated, anonymized analytics data from a sample of 3,700 mobile sites, March 2016. The flyer specifies visits, past tense, and the original data year rather than treating it as a universal current forecast.

## Investment arithmetic

The example now uses Foundation's actual $1,000 USD starting price, verified in `src/content/pricing.ts`; final scope is quoted separately. At the stated profit per additional customer, 4 x $250, 2 x $500, and 1 x $1,000 each equal $1,000. The example assumes profit after delivery costs and excludes website running costs. It does not promise additional customers or earnings. Version v5 replaces the earlier illustrative $1,500 investment at the user's request to align the example with an advertised starting price.

## Retained selling points

All seven original benefit themes are present: professional appearance, showcasing work, trust before a call, clear services, mobile usability, easy calling or estimate requests, and a reason to choose the business. The referral/comparison paragraph and the opportunity/high-value-project explanations are restored in concise wording.
