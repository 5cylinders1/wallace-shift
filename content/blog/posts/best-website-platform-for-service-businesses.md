## Choose around the work your website needs to do

A website platform is a long-term working environment for your business. Choosing it involves more than liking a template or being told a product is good for SEO.

For a service business, the site may need to explain what you do, establish whether a customer is a good fit, show credible evidence, and get an inquiry to someone who will respond. Some businesses also need booking, payments, estimates, or a customer account area. Those are different requirements and may involve different systems.

Our recommendation is to start with the smallest set of tools that can demonstrate the full journey and remain manageable after launch. That may be a hosted builder, a WordPress site, or custom development. There is no reason to pay for flexibility your business cannot yet use, or to accept a documented limitation that blocks essential work.

Wallace Shift builds custom software and websites. That perspective shapes the questions below, but it does not make custom development the right answer for every business.

## Write down the jobs before the platform names

Begin with the three actions a visitor most needs to complete. Be precise about the difference between information and transactions.

“Read our service area” is a content requirement. “Check whether this address is eligible and calculate an estimate” may need rules and data. “Request an appointment” is different from reserving an available appointment that immediately updates a working calendar.

Then describe the team's side:

- Who updates service descriptions, photos, hours, and articles?
- Which information should come from another system?
- Where should inquiries go, and who owns the response?
- Which changes require approval before publication?
- Who will diagnose a broken page or failed form?

If the basic customer journey is still unclear, work through [what a service website needs to support more inquiries](/blog/what-your-service-website-must-have-to-get-more-inquiries) first. A platform decision cannot substitute for a clear offer and a working contact path.

Separate launch requirements from possible future ideas. A feature becomes a requirement when you can explain its use, owner, and consequence if it is missing.

## Build a practical shortlist

Use the following options as starting points for evaluation. The “consider” column describes a reason to investigate, not a guarantee that a specific plan meets your needs.

| Option | Consider it when | Prove before choosing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Squarespace | You want to assess a hosted site builder for a defined marketing site | The editor suits your team and required integrations work on the chosen plan |
| Wix | You want to assess a hosted builder and its business tools together | The exact booking or inquiry flow works and its hosting model is acceptable |
| Webflow | You want to assess designed layouts with structured content | Routine editing, CMS structure, form delivery, and handover are practical |
| WordPress | You want to assess a site assembled around a chosen host, theme, and extensions | The complete configuration has a clear maintenance and recovery owner |
| Custom development | You have specific requirements that deserve a tailored implementation | Scope, editing, integrations, support, and ownership are defined in writing |

### Hosted builders: test your needs inside the product

Squarespace and Wix belong on the shortlist for an owner evaluating a hosted builder. Their suitability should be established with the pages and workflows you need, not a general claim that a builder is only for beginners.

Some boundaries matter early. Squarespace supports several custom-code approaches, but does not run your own server-side application code inside the platform. [Squarespace custom-code documentation](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/205815928-Add-custom-code-to-your-site)

Wix documents that its sites operate on Wix's infrastructure. Owning your content or managing your domain elsewhere is a different question from being able to move the working site to another host. [Wix's hosting and export explanation](https://support.wix.com/en/article/can-i-host-a-wix-site-on)

Neither point makes the product unsuitable. They help you decide whether the operating model fits your plans.

### Webflow: evaluate the content model as well as the design

For repeated content such as articles or team profiles, Webflow uses CMS Collections with shared fields and templates. [Webflow's CMS introduction](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961307099027-Intro-to-the-Webflow-CMS)

In a demonstration, ask someone to add the next real item rather than merely replace text on a finished page. Check whether the fields prompt them for useful information and whether the result stays readable with a longer title or missing optional image.

### WordPress: compare complete configurations

WordPress.com includes managed hosting, while independently hosted WordPress uses a hosting provider you choose. Specify which configuration a proposal means before comparing responsibility or cost. [WordPress.com and WordPress.org explained](https://wordpress.com/support/com-vs-org/)

For an independently hosted proposal, ask for the host, theme, major extensions, editor, and maintenance arrangement. Two proposals labeled “WordPress” can create very different daily experiences.

### Custom development: define the service behind the code

A custom website can be scoped around a particular business journey. It still needs a way to publish changes, operate integrations, and recover from problems.

Ask what is included in the build and what remains a separate service. If the owner wants to edit pages independently, include that requirement explicitly. If a customer portal is proposed, identify the information it exposes and the system responsible for keeping it current. The word “custom” does not settle those questions.

## Judge SEO through the site you can publish

A platform label is an inadequate SEO acceptance test. Evaluate whether you can publish useful, accessible pages with clear headings and links, descriptive titles, sensible URLs, and accurate information.

Google's guidance emphasizes helping search engines understand content and helping people find and use it. It also states that there is no method that automatically guarantees first-place rankings. [Google's SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)

Ask the proposed builder to demonstrate your service page, an article, a changed URL, and the controls that determine whether a page is indexable. Then assign responsibility for keeping the information current.

A business publishing useful answers consistently has a different operational requirement from one updating a five-page brochure twice a year. Choose an editing process that supports the work you intend to do, not a promise attached to the software brand.

## Compare the cost of keeping it useful

Use the same period and scope for every proposal. Include the initial build, hosting or platform subscription, domain, required apps, content migration, routine changes, maintenance, and support.

Ask what causes additional charges. Possibilities to investigate include more editors, higher usage, another integration, or a new type of page. Do not assume every listed feature is included in the plan being quoted.

Also ask who controls the business accounts. Domain access, billing, site administration, and connected services should have an agreed ownership arrangement. Record how access would be transferred if the working relationship ended.

Portability deserves a practical check. Squarespace's content export has exclusions, including style settings and custom CSS. Webflow code export also has limits: it does not reproduce the working CMS or form-processing service. Neither export should be mistaken for a complete replacement deployment. [Squarespace export details](https://support.squarespace.com/hc/en-us/articles/206566687-Export-your-site-content), [Webflow export details](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961386739347-How-do-I-export-my-Webflow-site-code)

## Run a small trial with real requirements

Imagine, hypothetically, a service company that wants to publish two kinds of work, show its service area, add occasional case examples, and route inquiries to an office manager. It does not currently need customer accounts or automated pricing.

Its trial could be one service page, one case example, and one contact form using safe test details. The office manager should try changing a phone number, adding a project image, and locating a test inquiry.

The team would record what worked, what required help, and what was missing. It would not add a portal merely because a demonstration made one look impressive. If eligibility rules later became important, that would become a new requirement to evaluate.

For an existing site, keep the useful parts in view. Our [website redesign prioritization guide](/blog/website-redesign-strategy-what-to-prioritize-first) can help distinguish a repair from a larger change.

## Copy this website platform requirements card

Complete this before requesting estimates. Give the same card to each candidate provider.

| Decision | Your entry should contain |
| --- | --- |
| Customer jobs | The three actions visitors must be able to complete |
| Required content | Service pages, service area, examples, articles, and other confirmed needs |
| Inquiry destination | System or inbox, responsible person, and next action |
| Editing owner | Named person and the changes they must make independently |
| Essential integration | Exact source, destination, fields, and desired result |
| Maintenance | Who checks updates, failures, backups, and recovery |
| Trial | One real page and one complete inquiry path to demonstrate |
| Cost comparison | Same period, included services, exclusions, and possible extra charges |
| Account ownership | Domain, platform, connected tools, and handover arrangements |
| Exit plan | What can move, what needs rebuilding, and how useful URLs are preserved |
| Decision | Chosen option, supporting evidence, and unresolved limitations |

Mark an item essential only when the business would reject a proposal without it. That keeps minor preferences from outweighing a failed contact flow or an editing process nobody will use.

## Common questions

### Is custom development always better for a growing business?

No. Growth should produce specific requirements to assess. A working site with manageable editing may remain suitable. Consider a different approach when you can demonstrate what the current setup prevents or makes unnecessarily difficult.

### Should I choose the cheapest platform?

Use total scope and operating responsibility to compare cost. A low subscription with an unsupported integration may be less practical than a different setup. Equally, a complex build can be unnecessary for straightforward requirements.

### Can I change platforms later?

Plan for that possibility, but investigate the actual export, integration, and URL work. Moving content is different from recreating the design and behavior. Keep an inventory of important pages, assets, accounts, and connections.
