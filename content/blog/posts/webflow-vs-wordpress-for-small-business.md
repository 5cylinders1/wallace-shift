## Compare the website you will operate after launch

A small business choosing between Webflow and WordPress needs more than a design comparison. Someone will update service details, publish an article, investigate a missing inquiry, and decide what to do when a change goes wrong.

Our recommendation is to compare the complete proposed setups through those everyday tasks. A well-built site with clear ownership is a different proposition from a demonstration that only its creator understands.

This guide uses a Webflow-hosted website and independently hosted WordPress as the main comparison. WordPress.com is a separate managed-hosting option; it should be evaluated under its own plan and service terms rather than assumed to have the same operating arrangement. [WordPress.com versus WordPress.org](https://wordpress.com/support/com-vs-org/)

If your shortlist is still open, first [compare website platforms against your business requirements](/blog/best-website-platform-for-service-businesses).

Wallace Shift builds custom websites and software. The framework below is intended to help you assess either proposal without assuming a custom build or a particular platform is automatically preferable.

## Define both proposals before scoring them

Ask each provider to describe what you would actually receive. For Webflow, identify the site plan, editing roles, CMS structure, required apps, and custom code. For WordPress, identify the host, theme, editor, important plugins, and maintenance arrangement.

Then give both providers the same requirements: the same services, number of content types, inquiry process, publishing needs, and expected changes. Otherwise, you may be comparing a straightforward marketing site with a much broader system.

Use a shared baseline:

| Decision | Question for both proposals |
| --- | --- |
| Content | What pages and repeated content types are included? |
| Editing | Which changes can our named editor make independently? |
| Design | Who changes layout and what requires additional work? |
| Inquiries | Where does the submitted information go and who acts on it? |
| Maintenance | Who checks changes, connections, and failures? |
| Recovery | What can be restored, by whom, and how is the result tested? |
| Ownership | Which accounts and assets does the business control? |
| Cost | What is included over the same period, and what is extra? |

Do not reduce the comparison to a feature count. One unsupported requirement can matter more than several optional capabilities.

## Test the actual editing experience

Webflow provides a content-editor role for updating copy, media, and CMS content without changing the underlying design. Publishing access can be controlled, and that role has limits on structural and design changes. [Webflow's content-editor documentation](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961251014931-Edit-site-content-as-a-content-editor)

That distinction can be useful if the business wants defined content changes while a designer owns layout. It can also frustrate someone who expects to restructure pages independently. Ask the intended editor to demonstrate the changes they will need, using their proposed role rather than the designer's account.

WordPress's editing experience depends on the setup. Its Site Editor can edit site-wide elements with blocks when a block theme is active. A proposal using a different theme or editing system should demonstrate that specific experience. [WordPress Site Editor documentation](https://wordpress.org/documentation/article/site-editor/)

Give the editor a short task list: change a service description, replace an image, update a phone number, and preview the result on a phone. Observe where they need help. The result is evidence about this configuration and this user, not a universal statement that one platform is easier.

## Design the content structure before adding pages

Think about what repeats. Articles, team profiles, service pages, and project examples may each need a consistent set of fields. Other pages may need a more flexible layout.

Webflow CMS Collections organize a content type around shared fields and a page template. The Collection structure determines what information an editor enters for each item. [Webflow's Collection documentation](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961244391059-Manage-CMS-Collections)

For a WordPress proposal, ask the builder to explain how ordinary pages, posts, and any additional content types will be modeled. If plugins are involved, ask what each adds. WordPress describes plugins as extensions that add or change functionality; their presence is part of the system you are choosing. [WordPress plugin documentation](https://wordpress.org/documentation/article/manage-plugins/)

Test awkward content as well as the ideal example. Use a longer title, a short description, and an item without an optional image. Confirm that the editor understands which fields are required and that the page remains coherent.

Good content structure reduces guesswork for the person maintaining the site. It still needs to be designed for your business, whichever platform is chosen.

## Follow a form submission all the way through

A contact form is a business process. Compare what happens after the visitor clicks submit, including confirmation, storage, delivery, assignment, and recovery.

Webflow forms can send submissions to configured destinations such as Webflow storage, email notifications, webhooks, and connected apps. A custom action uses a different configuration and cannot be combined with Webflow storage or email notifications in the same way. [Webflow's form documentation](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961347548563-How-do-I-add-forms-in-Webflow)

On a WordPress proposal, ask for the exact form solution and destination rather than assuming the platform name establishes delivery behavior. Check the documentation and account requirements for that particular setup.

For both, send recognizable test details and verify the actual destination. Test an invalid entry and inspect the error message. Confirm who can find a submission if an email does not arrive. Then identify who will notice a disconnected integration.

Use our [service website inquiry guide](/blog/what-your-service-website-must-have-to-get-more-inquiries) to review the surrounding customer journey. Changing platforms is not a substitute for assigning the next action.

## Assign maintenance and recovery explicitly

Ask for names and responsibilities, not “maintenance included.” A platform can provide a recovery feature while the business still needs someone to use it correctly.

Webflow creates backup restore points and supports restoring previous site versions. Its documentation notes that some settings and data are affected by restoration. Ask the maintainer to explain the relevant follow-up checks for your site. [Webflow backup and restore documentation](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961244069395-Save-and-restore-backups)

For WordPress, determine who maintains the host, theme, and plugins. WordPress recommends having a current backup before plugin updates. Its backup guidance also explains that a typical full restoration requires both files and the database. [WordPress plugin updates](https://wordpress.org/documentation/article/manage-plugins/), [WordPress backup guidance](https://developer.wordpress.org/advanced-administration/security/backup/)

Have the maintainer demonstrate recovery in a safe environment. Ask what would happen to an inquiry received after the chosen backup point. A plan should distinguish restoring the website from preserving business information that arrived later.

Also record how a problem is reported, who responds, and what falls outside ongoing support. A business can outsource this work, but it still needs a clear agreement about who owns it.

## Compare portability and full operating cost

Ask what moving away would involve. Separate your domain, content, design, code, customer data, and third-party connections. They may have different owners and different transfer processes.

Webflow's code export is available through eligible paid Workspace plans, but the exported files do not include working CMS functionality or form processing. Treat export as a defined capability with exclusions rather than proof that the complete site can be moved unchanged. [Webflow code-export documentation](https://help.webflow.com/hc/en-us/articles/33961386739347-How-do-I-export-my-Webflow-site-code)

For WordPress, ask who controls the hosting account, files, database, theme licenses, and plugin accounts. Request a written handover scope. Access to software does not eliminate the work of moving connections, preserving useful URLs, and testing the new environment.

Compare build and operating costs over the same period. Include the platform or host, paid extensions, editing access, maintenance, expected changes, and support. Ask which assumptions could change the estimate. Avoid comparing a bare software subscription with a proposal that includes ongoing professional work.

## Use the same-site trial worksheet

The following is a practical evaluation exercise, not a report of testing we have performed. Run it on a safe sample site for each proposal using the person who will own the task.

| Task | Acceptance evidence | Record for each platform |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Update a service page | Correct copy and image appear in the intended layout | Person, help required, and result |
| Publish an article | Title, body, links, and preview are correct | Editing steps and publishing permissions |
| Change a contact detail | Every intended instance changes, with no unrelated edits | Locations checked and anything missed |
| Submit an inquiry | One complete request arrives at the proper destination | Storage, notification, owner, and next action |
| Change a URL | The agreed old address reaches the relevant new page | Configuration owner and observed result |
| Recover a change | The intended version is restored without losing track of inquiries | Recovery owner, limits, and follow-up checks |

After each task, mark the result as completed independently, completed with help, or blocked. Record the reason rather than inventing a general ease-of-use score.

Then discuss the pattern. If a content editor completes routine tasks but needs occasional layout help, that may be acceptable. If everyday changes require support that the business has not budgeted for, revise the proposal before deciding.

## Common questions

### Which platform is better for SEO?

Evaluate the pages and publishing process rather than promising results from the platform name. Google emphasizes useful, understandable content and does not offer automatic ranking guarantees. Ask each provider to demonstrate the relevant page controls and implementation. [Google's SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)

### Does WordPress require me to do technical maintenance myself?

You can assign maintenance to a provider. The important question is what that provider has agreed to handle, including updates, connections, backups, recovery, and support. Get those responsibilities in writing rather than assuming they come with hosting.

### Will Webflow mean we never need a developer?

Evaluate the changes you expect. A content editor's permitted tasks differ from design, structural changes, or custom integration work. Use the trial to identify the assistance your proposed site will require and include it in the operating plan.
