If you have Johnson County construction projects in your pipeline for early 2027, now is a good time to check which jurisdiction handles each job and where your team keeps the answers.

Johnson County says its Board of County Commissioners will consider adopting the 2024 International Codes and county amendments on November 12, 2026. If adopted, the stated effective date is January 1, 2027. This remains a proposal as of October 28, 2026, when the county notice was checked for this article. [Johnson County code update notice](https://www.jocogov.org/department/building-codes/code-regulation-building-and-construction).

For contractors, the immediate administrative task is to identify the jobs that may need a closer look. A custom CRM could help organize that work, but a shared spreadsheet or your current system may be enough to start.

## Confirm the jurisdiction before changing a checklist

The county notice concerns properties in applicable unincorporated areas of Johnson County. Do not assume it applies to every project with a Johnson County address. The county provides an [address lookup](https://maps.jocogov.org/aimssubmit/servicerequests_pln.html) and directs users whose result names a city to that city's planning department.

Add the confirmed jurisdiction to each job record. Keep the verification date and source alongside it. If nobody has checked, use a status such as “jurisdiction not confirmed” rather than filling in a guess.

For work near the proposed transition, ask the responsible building department which requirements apply to that particular project. Keep the answer with the job. An estimated start date alone should not drive an automatic code decision.

## Give each open question an owner

A useful starting checklist for jobs planned around the new year is:

- Project address and confirmed permitting jurisdiction.
- Permit application and approval status, with relevant dates.
- Applicable code edition as confirmed for the project, or an unresolved question.
- Current plans, permit documents and correspondence.
- Person responsible for the next action and the date to check back.

These are suggested internal tracking fields, not a county submission checklist. Your estimator, office administrator and project manager should agree on what each status means before anyone builds an automation around it.

For example, “submitted” should mean the application was actually submitted. “Ready to submit” should remain a separate status. Attach or link the supporting record so the next person can see what happened without searching through several inboxes.

## Build the CRM around a real handoff

Consider a hypothetical remodel planned for early 2027. The estimator has prepared a scope, but the team has not confirmed which requirements apply to the permit. A workflow could look like this:

1. The office creates one job record with the address, customer, estimate and jurisdiction check.
2. An assigned person contacts the appropriate building department and records the question, response and date.
3. The project manager reviews that answer with the people responsible for plans and estimating, then records any changes needed.
4. A task goes to the owner of each change, with the relevant document attached or linked.
5. The office updates the customer after a person reviews any effect on scope, price or timing.

A custom CRM could be configured to show unresolved questions, remind assigned staff and retain a history of changes. Document access, permissions, integrations and notifications would need to be scoped and tested for your business. These are workflow design options, not a claim that a particular Wallace Shift product already includes them.

## Keep reminders separate from decisions

Start with internal reminders. A weekly view of jobs missing a jurisdiction check or waiting on a response may be more useful than another automated customer email.

If you add notifications, define when they stop. A canceled job should not keep producing follow-up tasks. A revised document should not silently replace the version the crew is using. Someone should review a failed integration rather than assume the information arrived.

## Start with the jobs already on your calendar

Review your active estimates and upcoming jobs. Identify the records missing a confirmed jurisdiction, a clear next action or the latest documents. Fix one recurring handoff before committing to a larger system.
