# Wallace Shift homepage modification plan

These three mockups reorganize the homepage inside its current visual identity. They preserve the logo, Manrope and Instrument Serif typography, ink and pearl surfaces, azure controls, restrained borders and generous spacing. They are review concepts, not published pages or proven conversion improvements.

Recruiting research participants is not a prerequisite. We can address observed gaps now, then learn from real inquiries as traffic grows.

## Three directions

| Direction | Opening experience | Section order | Strength | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Offers first | A concise service explanation beside the $250 monthly website and $1,500 lead page offer | Opening offers, custom systems link, website and follow up examples, process, questions, optional showcase, contact | Buyers see what they can buy and what it costs immediately | Gives the focused offers more emphasis than custom systems |
| Examples first | A website demonstration leads visually, with both offer prices visible in the opening area | Opening example and price strip, offer comparison and custom systems link, follow up demonstration, process, optional showcase, questions, contact | Lets the work explain the service before a detailed comparison | Examples must clearly distinguish included features from additional work |
| Choose by need | Three visible paths: improve my website, turn campaign inquiries into a clear next step, connect my business systems | Need selection with prices, website and follow up examples, process, questions, optional showcase, contact | Gives website and custom system buyers a recognizable starting point | Adds a small self selection decision before detailed information |

All three keep prices visible, allow direct browsing and provide a question route. None uses a quiz, readiness threshold or forced animation to unlock information.

**Recommended starting point: Offers first.** It makes the specific $250 and $1,500 purchases easiest to find while retaining a clear custom systems route. Choose by need is the stronger alternative if the homepage must give custom CRM and workflow projects equal prominence. This recommendation is based on observed information gaps, not competitor conversion data.

## Homepage changes after a direction is selected

1. Replace the opening information hierarchy. Introduce websites, lead pages and connected business systems in plain language. Show the two offer prices with their billing cadence. Keep a visible route for custom CRM, AI and workflow work.
2. Place useful buying information in normal page flow. Headings, prices and controls must remain readable and usable without the animation loading or completing.
3. Bring examples close to the opening. Reuse existing website and follow up demonstrations. Label illustrative businesses as demos and explain that advanced demo features may require separate scope.
4. Explain benefits through concrete deliverables. Show how a website presents services and enables contact, and how the lead page collects an inquiry and triggers an agreed response. Make no unsupported lead or revenue promises.
5. Reduce repeated service introductions. Give each visitor one overview, then relevant detail. Preserve the broader capabilities through a concise custom systems section instead of placing every engagement at the same decision level.
6. Present a short process: understand the need, agree the scope, build and launch. State what the customer provides and what happens after an inquiry.
7. End with useful answers and an open conversation. Offer “Ask a question” alongside the offer specific actions. Carry the chosen service into the inquiry rather than sending website buyers to an unrelated AI or CRM heading.

## Relationship to pricing and offer pages

The homepage introduces the choices. `/pricing` compares scope, costs and terms. `/offers/contractor-websites` and `/offers/lead-page-follow-up` provide the corresponding demos, detailed scope and inquiry path. Ads that promise one of these offers should link directly to its offer page.

The two offer pages are local work at the time of this review. Publishing and integrating a selected design would be a separate implementation step.

Before publication, resolve website page allowance, content responsibilities, maintenance limits, ownership, minimum commitment and cancellation. Specify the lead workflow's supported channels, compatibility and any recurring hosting, software or message costs. Do not invent favorable terms to make a mockup persuasive. Distinguish the existing Foundation page and follow up setup from the two new packages.

## Preserve the animation

Keep the complete current experience intact on an optional showcase route. Link to it through a still preview in the homepage's work section and a footer link. Load the interactive scene only when requested. This preserves its role as a demonstration of creative and technical capability while removing any dependency between buying information and scene readiness. The current homepage stays intact during this mockup review.

## Competitor patterns used

[Footbridge](https://www.footbridgemedia.com/) packages a broad set of services into a clear main purchase. [180 Sites](https://180sites.com/pricing/) makes packages and prices easy to compare; its [agreement](https://get.180sites.com/180-sites-agreement/) shows why commitment and ownership must accompany headline prices. [CGN's verified ad destination](https://www.contractorgrowthnetwork.com/results/remodeling-company-seo-results/) uses concrete work and explanation before inquiry. Its [webinar destination](https://www.contractorgrowthnetwork.com/webinar/google-ai-remodeler/) accommodates people still learning. These are observed patterns, not evidence of their acquisition profitability.

## Verification without a testing service

Check every mockup on desktop and mobile for readable prices, working paths, keyboard access and unobstructed questions. After implementation, record inquiry source, selected offer, questions, qualification and eventual outcome. Note recurring misunderstandings and revise the relevant explanation. Treat low volume trends as directional; clicks and usability checks do not establish increased sales.

Acceptance criteria for the first production experiment:

* Buying information renders immediately, without an animation or loading sequence blocking access.
* Both $250/month and $1,500 once appear in the opening offer area with their cadence and a route to scope and terms.
* Desktop keyboard and mobile journeys reach each offer, its details and an inquiry without clipped controls or dead ends.
* Visitors can ask questions without selecting a package or declaring purchase readiness.
* Record relevant visitors and qualified inquiries using the same definitions before and after the change. Inspect misunderstandings and sales outcomes; report small samples as observations, not statistical proof of improvement.
