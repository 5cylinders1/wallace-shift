## Start here

I want you to assess Wallace Shift and recommend how we should move forward on Instagram. Previous reels, captions, and posting plans are intentionally not included in this handoff. Do not seek them out or use them as the creative baseline unless I ask you to.

Understand the business, evaluate the audience and available evidence, and recommend a direction. You have room to propose a different visual style, content mix, structure, and posting cadence. Explain how your choices help attract relevant followers and prospective clients. Do not assume our previous approach or a particular number of daily posts should continue.

The business facts and explicit preferences below are established. Items marked **Proposed default** or **Working principle** are starting points you may assess and refine with me.

* **Name:** Matthew Wallace.
* **Business:** Wallace Shift.
* **Handle:** @wallaceshift.
* **Website:** https://wallaceshift.com
* **What I do, in my words:** I help people save money and time by integrating AI into their business.

## What I sound like

* **Three of my own reels or captions that sound most like me:** Not supplied intentionally. Assess the direction independently and develop new drafts for me to review. Do not invent examples and present them as things I have said or published.
* **Overall voice:** Plain, direct, conversational, and grounded. Sound like a person who understands the work and can explain it clearly to a business owner.
* **On camera I am:** Proposed default: calm, candid, and conversational. My actual delivery should be learned from a recording I supply, not inferred from a written brief.

* **Emoji in captions:** Proposed default: minimal, usually none. Use one only when it helps the message.
* **Face on camera:** Mostly screen demos and voiceover, with occasional camera appearances. This is my confirmed preference.
* **Voiceover or to camera:** Voiceover is the primary spoken format. Use camera appearances when a personal explanation or point of view would help. Do not assume permission to clone my voice or likeness.
* **Pace:** My speaking speed has not been measured. If a production tool requires a number, use 160 words per minute as a temporary planning estimate and revise it after timing the actual read. Leave room for viewers to understand the screen.

### Writing rules

* Never add hyphens or dash punctuation to public copy. Use periods, commas, or separate sentences. File paths and technical identifiers in internal notes are exempt.
* Use natural contractions and concrete examples. Read scripts aloud before presenting them.
* Prefer short, connected sentences. Avoid corporate language, forced slang, generic motivational copy, and exaggerated urgency.
* Talk about the work the owner recognizes before introducing a tool or technical term.
* Explain CRM briefly when the audience needs it. Use "CRM integrated with AI" when that is the accurate description.
* Use "I" for Matthew's own experience and opinions. Use "we" for Wallace Shift's services. Do not invent a personal story or imply a larger team to support the copy.
* Do not make every post a sales pitch or force the same slogan into every ending.
* Keep all proposed scripts and captions open to revision after my feedback. This brief is not evidence of my natural speaking rhythm.

## My positions

These are working principles consistent with the current offer. Develop them into specific, defensible ideas and let me review any strong opinion attributed to me.

1. The useful starting point is a real problem in the business. Choose the technology after understanding the work.
2. AI should have a clear job, relevant information, and appropriate human review. A straightforward rule or existing feature may be the better solution.
3. A useful system makes the next action and responsible person clear. More features do not automatically make it more useful.
4. Check what the tools a business already uses can do before recommending a replacement or a custom build.
5. A website should help people understand the offer and take the next step. The way an inquiry reaches the team deserves attention alongside the design.

## Off limits

* **Topics I do not post about:** Proposed editorial boundary: keep the account relevant to the business and its owners. Avoid unrelated controversy, personal attacks, fear about AI replacing everyone, and get rich quick content. Ask before introducing personal or sensitive stories.
* **Clients, numbers, or names I cannot say publicly:** This file grants no permission to name a client, show private records, or publish customer messages. Obtain permission for the specific material. Use fictional sample data in demonstrations and label it clearly.
* **Claims I am not allowed to make:** Do not invent client results, testimonials, credentials, partnerships, integrations, revenue, savings, audience growth, or conversion figures. Do not guarantee leads, income, accuracy, or outcomes.
* **AI and automation claims:** Show the actual task and explain where a person reviews or decides. Do not describe an interface concept as a functioning integration or a local demonstration as a live client system.
* **Business commitments:** Do not invent prices, discounts, availability, delivery dates, free services, or support promises. Check current scope and terms before drafting an offer.

## Proof I can use

No approved client outcome figures or testimonials are supplied in this brief. That is a content constraint, not a reason to insert invented numbers or leave placeholders in a finished post.

* **Business facts:** Use the current services and scope in the repository as the starting source. These establish what we offer, not results achieved for a client.
* **Product evidence:** Independently assess suitable websites, applications, or workflows we can show. Verify the specific behavior before describing it. Confirm the right to show the material and label demos and sample data.
* **Educational evidence:** A clear explanation, a reproducible check, or an honestly labeled example can be useful without a client outcome claim.
* **Personal experience:** Use only experiences I actually provide or confirm. Do not invent anecdotes to make a script sound more human.
* **Measured results:** Before using a number, record its source, date range, what was measured, and permission to share it. Keep illustrative calculations distinct from actual savings.
* **Missing proof:** Rewrite around an observable fact or ask for the exact evidence needed. Do not put unresolved placeholders into content labeled ready to publish.

## The ask

* **Business objective:** Start useful conversations with owners who have a real operational problem we could help solve.
* **Call to action:** Choose one action that fits the post. It could be trying a practical check, describing a recurring problem, following for relevant content, or contacting Wallace Shift about a project.
* **Tone:** Direct and low pressure. Make it clear why taking the next step is useful.
* **Resource offers:** Do not promise to send a checklist, audit, or other resource until it exists and we are ready to deliver it. Do not imply a comment or DM automation is running unless verified.
* **Current internal idea:** The Lead Follow Up Check in the knowledge documents is a proposed pilot, not an offer automatically approved for promotion. Assess it and confirm delivery readiness with me before advertising it.
* **Contact destination:** Use wallaceshift.com when appropriate. Verify the actual destination and current profile link before saying "link in bio."

## How I want you to assess the account

1. Review the current business, services, and scope. Identify the strongest audience and the specific problems our content could help them understand.
2. If current account access or analytics are available and authorized, assess them. State what you actually reviewed. Do not infer account performance from the existence of saved assets or assume a post has been published.

7. Define how we will judge the experiment. Consider relevant reach, viewing behavior, saves, profile activity, and qualified conversations where data is available. Keep attention metrics separate from business results.

## Production handoff

Once we agree on a direction, each post package should include the concept and purpose, exact script or on screen copy, caption, cover, necessary disclosure, finished asset, and a short verification note. Keep source files editable.

Check readability on a phone, caption accuracy, audio and video timing, and the actual exported file. State whether something is a draft, exported, scheduled, or published. A saved file is not proof of publication.

Recommend music we can actually use for the intended account and placement. Verify licensing or account availability before treating a track as cleared.

Prepare drafts and assets within the task I authorize. Obtain explicit authorization before publishing, scheduling, spending credits, or sending messages. Do not treat this voice guide as authorization for those actions.
