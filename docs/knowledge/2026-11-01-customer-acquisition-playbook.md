# Wallace Shift customer acquisition playbook

Prepared November 1, 2026. Internal recommendations, ready to test. No campaign has been launched through this document.

Follow-on direction: the [AI integration offer and acquisition plan](2026-11-01-paid-acquisition-funnel-plan.md) uses a universal time-saving message, a provisional free website bonus with projects of $2,500 or more, and a $1,000 to $3,000 monthly ad budget. Paid acquisition runs alongside near-term conversations with existing opportunities. The discovery and delivery guidance below support that plan; roofing is no longer the selected audience.

The first experiment to run: offer a **Lead Follow Up Check** to one group of service business owners. Give each suitable prospect a short map of their inquiry process, any gap we can confirm, and one practical next step. Use the findings to determine whether they need a simple setup, a connected workflow, or a custom system.

## What this means for Wallace Shift

Wallace Shift already offers custom CRMs, AI integrations, workflow automation, and supporting websites. Its [service content](../../src/content/servicesPage.ts) names concrete problems: inquiries split across inboxes, estimates in spreadsheets, repeated data entry, and next steps held in someone's head.

Use that language in sales conversations. A useful starting message is:

> We help small teams keep track of inquiries, follow up on estimates, and see what needs attention. We start with the tools you already use and build around the parts that are getting missed.

When AI is relevant, name the task: drafting a reply, summarizing job notes, or finding information in business documents. Explain who reviews the output. “CRM integrated with AI” describes the system; the specific task explains why the buyer might need it.

For the first experiment, choose one trade where we can reach owners and understand their process. Roofing is a reasonable candidate because the repository already contains a [sample workflow menu](../../src/demos/roofers-offer/automationMenu.ts). That is a readiness advantage, not evidence that roofing demand has been validated.

## Entry offer: Lead Follow Up Check

**Buyer:** an owner or operations lead whose team handles inquiries and estimates across several people or tools.

**Question answered:** Where does the next action become unclear after someone asks for a quote?

**Proposed format:** a short conversation plus a one-page finding. Reserve up to 30 minutes of total preparation, conversation, and write-up per review for the pilot. If that proves insufficient, reduce the scope or adjust the capacity estimate.

**Inputs:** website, current tools, the person responsible for inquiries, and one anonymized recent example. A website review alone cannot establish what happens inside the business.

**Deliverable:** inquiry path, one observed gap or an explicit “no gap confirmed,” an immediate action the owner can take, and an optional implementation recommendation. The [worksheet](lead-follow-up-check.md) is ready to use.

**Boundary:** no production access, installation, full process audit, or custom prototype is needed for this check. Broader investigation belongs in paid discovery. The free result is still useful if the owner buys nothing.

**Qualification:** confirm an actual recurring problem, access to someone who understands the process, and willingness to discuss a practical next step. A checklist is sufficient for people still exploring. Do not assume every business needs new software.

Proposed invitation, for use only when we are ready to deliver the check:

> What happens after someone asks you for a quote? Send us your website and tell us how your team handles the next step. We'll review that process with you and return a short map, any gap we can confirm, and a practical next action.

## Route the finding to an appropriate service

These are current repository starting points as of November 1, 2026, from [pricing.ts](../../src/content/pricing.ts). Confirm scope and current terms before quoting. This is a routing guide, not a compulsory sequence of purchases.

| Finding | Suitable next step | Current starting point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One reminder or sequence can run inside an existing tool | Follow-up setup | From $500; budget $500 to $1,000 for a small setup |
| Information must pass between tools | Connected workflow | From $2,500, subject to access and scope |
| Several possible causes require investigation | Automation audit | $1,000; optional for clearly defined setups |
| One workflow needs a shared portal, dashboard, or internal tool | Focused system build | From $5,000; up to five main views, two roles, one straightforward integration |
| A larger system has unclear requirements | System definition sprint | $2,500 before a separately scoped build |
| The team needs AI to interpret or draft information | Focused AI integration | Separate scope, testing, usage costs, and quote |

Software fees, cleanup, migration, complex AI, and additional integrations are not implicitly included. Support is a separate discussion. The older $18,000 focused-build figure in `docs/software-positioning.md` conflicts with current pricing content and should not be used for this experiment.

## Turn current work into usable sales material

Create one short demonstration of an inquiry becoming an assigned task with a visible next action. Use an existing relevant demo if it actually shows that sequence. Add a visible sample-data label. A functioning interface can demonstrate behavior; it does not establish customer savings or revenue.

Suggested opening:

> This is a sample roofing workflow. I'll show you where an inquiry lands, who owns the next step, and how an overdue estimate gets noticed.

Prepare these four supporting pieces from that same workflow:

| Buyer question | Useful material |
| --- | --- |
| “Where are we losing track?” | The blank Lead Follow Up Check worksheet |
| “Could our current software handle this?” | A comparison of configuration, connection, and custom build for that specific process |
| “What would your work look like?” | A labeled demonstration and a sample scope |
| “What happens when something fails?” | A concrete explanation of alerts, ownership, recovery, and support boundaries |

For real projects, keep a small evidence record: original problem, delivered scope, acceptance evidence, measurement period, any measured change, and permission to publish. Separate technical acceptance from business outcomes. Ask for candid feedback; a favorable review is not a condition of receiving work.

## A manageable 30 day experiment

The quantities below are proposed workload limits, not conversion predictions. Confirm capacity before offering delivery dates. Use one primary channel: personal conversations with suitable owners already in our network or reachable through an introduction.

| Period | Work | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1 to 3 | Choose one trade and workflow; prepare the worksheet and one existing demonstration | One clear offer and a defined delivery limit |
| Days 4 to 10 | Invite 10 suitable owners individually; record the context and response | A first set of language, fit, and objection observations |
| Days 11 to 20 | Complete up to five checks; offer a written scope where appropriate | Findings, time spent, next steps, and reasons for declining |
| Days 21 to 30 | Follow through on agreed next steps; review the results; change one weak part | A decision to continue, revise, or stop this offer |

Do not build another demo solely to begin this test. Check whether an existing one answers the buyer's question. Where a working example is missing, a clearly labeled process diagram can explain the proposal.

One possible title variant for a later batch is **Know What Happens After Every Inquiry**. A handful of conversations can reveal confusing wording; it cannot establish a reliable statistical winner. Expand only after there is evidence of fit and enough capacity to serve the work.

## Record the numbers that support a decision

Track each prospect's source, trade, problem, current tools, invitation date, response, check status, fit, agreed next action, proposal, outcome, and delivery minutes. Record why a prospect declined or did not qualify.

| Measure | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Response rate | Substantive responses / delivered invitations |
| Qualified check rate | Completed checks with a confirmed recurring problem and an appropriate decision maker / completed checks |
| Proposal acceptance | Accepted proposals / proposals with a final decision; report pending proposals separately |
| Review effort | Total review delivery minutes / completed checks |
| Acquisition cost | (Attributable acquisition spending + acquisition hours × agreed internal hourly cost) / new paying clients. Include free-check preparation, conversation, write-up, and follow-through. |
| Project contribution | For completed projects: collected project revenue less direct delivery costs and delivery time at an agreed internal hourly cost. For active projects, also deduct estimated remaining delivery costs and label the result provisional. |

Define the date range and keep the underlying counts beside each percentage. If there are no paying clients, report acquisition spend and zero wins; do not report a misleading zero acquisition cost. Allow for the time between a conversation and a purchase.

Use the pattern to decide the next change:

- Few responses: check relevance, reachability, and whether the invitation is understandable.
- Reviews completed but few suitable problems: change the audience or entry question.
- Suitable problems but no implementation interest: investigate urgency, trust, budget, and whether the existing tool already solves it.
- Proposals stalled: ask what remains unclear about scope, access, timing, or decision ownership.
- Work sold but delivery exceeds estimates: tighten scope and improve delivery before increasing volume.

## Delivery turns this into a repeatable service

For each paid implementation, agree the trigger, responsible person, required input, expected output, stop conditions, exceptions, and acceptance criteria. Test a normal case and the failures that matter for that workflow, such as duplicate inquiries, a missing address, or a reply that should stop follow-up.

For AI work, add representative examples, data-access rules, human review, and fallback behavior. Those expectations already appear in Wallace Shift's [service scope](../../src/content/servicesPage.ts). A successful demonstration is not production acceptance.

The first next action is to complete one worksheet against an anonymized process we understand, verify that the result is useful, and time the work. That establishes what we can reasonably offer before publishing the invitation.
