# Wallace Shift: next website-content opportunities

Research date: October 25, 2026, America/Chicago; final source check October 26, 2026 UTC. Local site reviewed at commit `cbef7e7`. This is a research and editorial recommendation, not a publication change.

## Recommendation

Prioritize three specific problems: **contact-form email delivery, inquiry measurement, and the handoff from a website form to a CRM**. They fit Wallace Shift's current website and connected-systems services, and the existing articles introduce them without providing complete implementation or troubleshooting guides.

There are two further candidates: **ongoing website maintenance budgeting** and **when a service business needs location pages**. Treat maintenance as conditional because the existing custom-website cost article already covers total ownership cost. Location-page planning has a distinct informational purpose, but its connection to Wallace Shift's present services is less direct than the first three topics.

Do not add another generic redesign, website-cost, platform-selection, or conversion guide. Update the appropriate existing article. Broad buying searches should also have a clear route to the services and pricing pages.

These are editorial priorities, not estimates of traffic, keyword difficulty, ranking potential, or guaranteed inquiries. The search evidence establishes that relevant publishers address these questions; it does not establish demand size or that Wallace Shift can outrank them.

## Method and business boundaries

I ran **12 distinct English-language search queries**, recorded below, and opened selected results from their original publishers. I compared agency and vendor pages with primary technical documentation and all 16 local published articles. This was a search-tool snapshot, not a controlled Google rank-tracking study with a fixed location, device, or personalized-search setting. Positions and result counts are not reported.

“Observed” below means visible in a reviewed page or local file. “Inferred” means an editorial opportunity suggested by that comparison; it remains a hypothesis until content, audience response, and search performance can be assessed.

The local sources [servicesPage.ts](/Users/matthewwallace/Repos/WallaceShiftLogoSite/src/content/servicesPage.ts), [about.ts](/Users/matthewwallace/Repos/WallaceShiftLogoSite/src/content/about.ts), and [pricing.ts](/Users/matthewwallace/Repos/WallaceShiftLogoSite/src/content/pricing.ts) establish these boundaries:

- Website work includes a clear offer, copy/design/development, inquiry handoff, documentation, and optional care. CRM integrations are explicitly scoped where needed.
- Automation starts with a **paid audit**. Systems work can require a paid definition sprint. Neither should be described as a free diagnostic service.
- Current positioning emphasizes custom CRMs, connected workflows, and practical AI integrations. That makes the connection between a public website and the team receiving an inquiry especially relevant.
- About describes remote work and possible meetings at a client's premises or a neutral location. It establishes **no particular city, staffed office, or public storefront**. Do not create a Wallace Shift city keyword strategy or claim Business Profile eligibility from these facts.
- Ongoing website care is offered. Done-for-you local SEO, advertising management, and Business Profile management are not established website-service inclusions in these sources.

## Candidate 1 — contact-form email delivery

**Proposed title:** Contact Form Says “Sent,” but No Email Arrives: A Troubleshooting Guide  
**Slug:** `contact-form-not-sending-email`  
**Priority:** First group.  
**Intent:** Urgent problem solving after a visitor submits a form; queries 3 and 4. Combine missing email and spam-delivery diagnosis in one article initially.

**Observed competition:** [Dobble's contact-form troubleshooting guide](https://dobble.com.au/learning-centre/why-is-my-website-contact-form-not-sending-emails) already covers different failure symptoms, controlled tests, mail transport, configuration, and delivery errors. [WPForms' spam-delivery article](https://wpforms.com/why-your-contact-form-emails-are-going-to-spam-how-to-fix-it/) provides a product-oriented troubleshooting path. [Google Workspace's contact-form guidance](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/support/troubleshooting/troubleshoot-gmail-not-getting-contact-form-messages) supplies authoritative Gmail-specific investigation steps. This is not an empty topic.

**Observed site gap:** The existing inquiry-diagnosis article tells readers to trace a test request and inspect missing notifications. It does not provide an incident worksheet, explain the possible handoff states in detail, or help a business owner prepare an actionable evidence packet for the person maintaining the site.

**Inferred angle:** A vendor-neutral explanation of where the request disappeared, with a clear division between checks the business can perform and checks requiring its site's administrator. The goal is a reproducible finding, not an immediate recommendation to install a particular mail plugin.

**Scope and original resource:** Create a **form-delivery incident worksheet** with test label, timestamp/time zone, page, submission-record evidence, mail-provider status/message ID where available, recipient mailbox, notification owner, result, and retest. A conceptual sequence should distinguish browser confirmation, saved submission, provider acceptance, mailbox delivery, and a human action. Use a controlled example with synthetic details, and label it as such unless a real demonstration is built and documented.

Include branches for no saved record, saved record but no notification attempt, rejected send, spam/filtering, and delivered message with no owner. A successful page or accepted send should not be presented as proof of every downstream step. Explain how to preserve an inquiry while investigating, with access and retention appropriate to the actual system. Do not supply universal DNS records, recommend disabling protection, or request credentials in a worksheet.

**Links and CTA:** Link to [inquiry diagnosis](https://wallaceshift.com/blog/why-your-website-is-not-generating-inquiries) for the broader problem, [website requirements](https://wallaceshift.com/blog/what-your-service-website-must-have-to-get-more-inquiries) for contact-path design, and [automation tools](https://wallaceshift.com/blog/small-business-automation-tools) where a connector is involved. One CTA: [describe the reproducible failure to Wallace Shift](https://wallaceshift.com/contact). Scope repair or integration work after reviewing the actual setup.

## Candidate 2 — verified inquiry measurement

**Proposed title:** How to Track Website Inquiries From Form Submission to Qualified Lead  
**Slug:** `track-website-inquiries-qualified-leads`  
**Priority:** First group.  
**Intent:** Set up and validate reporting that connects website actions with useful business inquiries; queries 1 and 2.

**Observed competition:** [Tucker Shively's GA4 and Tag Manager quickstart](https://tuckershively.com/docs/tracking/ga4-gtm-quickstart/) includes form events, phone and email clicks, verification, and troubleshooting. It already distinguishes phone taps from actual call outcomes. Other discovered results cover calls, WhatsApp, forms, and attribution. Merely explaining click tracking would provide limited differentiation.

**Observed site gap:** The diagnostic article already distinguishes event counts from received inquiries and includes a hypothetical mismatch. It does not provide a complete event-definition and reconciliation exercise readers can apply across their analytics and receiving records.

**Inferred angle:** Make the primary deliverable a trustworthy definition of a received and qualified inquiry, rather than a dashboard full of loosely named conversions. Treat analytics as one evidence source and reconcile it with a booking, inbox, CRM, or call record as appropriate.

**Scope and original resource:** Build an **event-to-inquiry reconciliation sheet**: channel, browser action, intended event, receiving record, duplicate rule, qualification rule, source when known, owner, controlled-test result, and reporting caveat. Include one hypothetical week with deliberately conflicting counts, then explain the unresolved differences instead of forcing the figures to agree.

Use [GA4 recommended events](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9267735?hl=en) for lead-stage terminology and [enhanced measurement documentation](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9216061?hl=en) for form-event behavior. Configuration still needs to be verified for the actual site. An event name is not a guarantee that the request reached the business. A tap to call is not a completed call, and an unknown source should remain unknown. Google prohibits sending personally identifiable information into Analytics; follow [its PII guidance](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/6366371?hl=en) without turning that product policy into a claim of general legal compliance.

**Overlap boundary:** The existing diagnosis article identifies whether measurement is suspect. This guide defines, implements at a conceptual level, and tests the measurement. Avoid another overview of all the reasons a website underperforms.

**Links and CTA:** Link to [inquiry diagnosis](https://wallaceshift.com/blog/why-your-website-is-not-generating-inquiries), [the website inquiry guide](https://wallaceshift.com/blog/what-your-service-website-must-have-to-get-more-inquiries), and [automation planning](https://wallaceshift.com/blog/save-time-with-automation-stop-losing-hours-to-busy-work). One CTA: [discuss the inquiry handoff and reporting requirement](https://wallaceshift.com/contact). Do not imply that full CRM attribution is automatically included in a basic website package.

## Candidate 3 — website form to CRM

**Proposed title:** How to Connect a Website Form to a CRM Without Losing or Duplicating Leads  
**Slug:** `connect-website-form-to-crm`  
**Priority:** First group, especially strong fit with current systems positioning.  
**Intent:** Implement and validate a specific connection; query 11.

**Observed competition:** [Coherence's website-form-to-CRM guide](https://getcoherence.io/blog/website-form-to-crm-lead-capture) already covers record types, field mapping, minimum information, native versus connector approaches, tests, and failure paths. [Hive's contact-form documentation](https://help.hive.com/en/articles/17602196-capture-leads-from-your-website-with-crm-contact-forms) explains its native form and CRM behavior. A generic “pick Zapier, map the fields, test it” article would add little.

**Observed site gap:** Existing automation articles introduce inquiry routing and tool evaluation. None walks a single pre-sale handoff through mapping, identity rules, assignment, a partial failure, and a safe retry.

**Inferred angle:** Publish an acceptance-tested handoff specification a business owner can use when buying or checking an integration. Where practical, build one small demonstration with synthetic records and show the resulting evidence. Do not claim a demonstration was tested until that work is actually completed.

**Scope and original resource:** Provide a **field-mapping and handoff acceptance workbook**. Include required/optional fields, authoritative source, normalization rule, record identity, create-versus-update behavior, ownership, next action, failure destination, retry rule, and test evidence. Cover new contact, known contact with a new inquiry, repeated submission, missing required data, revoked access, and contact created but follow-up task failed.

Do not collapse “duplicate contact” and “duplicate inquiry”: a returning person can have a legitimate new request. Compare native integration, managed connector, and custom endpoint by actual requirements and available access. Avoid a universal winner. Explain human review and recovery without promising exactly-once behavior from an unverified integration.

**Overlap boundary:** End at a verified lead record, responsible person, and next action. The [onboarding article](https://wallaceshift.com/blog/automate-client-onboarding) owns the workflow after agreement. The [tools article](https://wallaceshift.com/blog/small-business-automation-tools) owns broad product selection.

**Links and CTA:** Link to those two articles, [automation planning](https://wallaceshift.com/blog/save-time-with-automation-stop-losing-hours-to-busy-work), and [web app versus website](https://wallaceshift.com/blog/web-app-vs-website) for larger system scope. One CTA: [start with Wallace Shift's paid automation audit](https://wallaceshift.com/services#automation).

## What would improve these recommendations

Search Console query/page data could show whether an existing URL is already attracting one of these narrower questions. Actual questions from prospects or support inquiries could identify the most useful form platform, CRM, or failure mode to demonstrate. Neither dataset was available for this audit, so no recommendation depends on invented demand or customer history.

For the first three candidates, the strongest next work is a small controlled demonstration plus its evidence sheet. A troubleshooting flow that a reader can actually follow is a better reason to publish than matching another keyword variation. Use only synthetic records or properly authorized examples, and clearly separate hypothetical illustrations from observed tests.

Before publication, verify current vendor behavior and screenshots, all cited URLs, actual service scope, and the relevant internal link targets. Preserve each existing article's broad purpose and link out at the point where the reader needs the deeper procedure. Do not add planned article URLs to published bodies before those pages exist.
