## Current offer and boundaries

- **Automation:** a paid audit maps repetitive work, cost, and failure points and returns a prioritized plan. A build covers a defined workflow; more complex connections need explicit scope.
- **Managed automation:** monitoring, maintenance, failure recovery, and minor adjustments to existing workflows. New workflows and substantial changes are separate work.
- **AI:** agree the task, sample inputs, review criteria, and data handling before scoping implementation and provider costs. Do not imply complex AI systems are included in standard automation packages.
- **CRM/business systems:** start with a paid definition sprint, assess the current tools, and scope migration or integrations explicitly.
- **Roofing:** the roof-finish preview and CRM examples are illustrative concepts. They are not customer case studies, proof of ROI, measurements of an installed roof, or proof that every named vendor integration exists.

Use `/services#automation` and `/pricing#automation` for audit, workflow, and recovery articles; `/services#web-apps` and `/pricing#web-apps` for the roofing handoff system decision. `/contact` is appropriate for a scoped discussion. Do not offer a free audit or quietly promise that an integration is included in a website package.

## What the inspected pages actually establish

These are observations about page content, not acceptance of every claim made by the publisher.

- **Autometa:** already compares four connection approaches and supplies field mapping and a test runbook. A generic “connect your form” guide would add little. Its examples also make identifier assumptions; Wallace Shift can examine contact, property, and job identity separately. [Inspected page](https://www.autometa.in/blog/how-to-send-website-form-submissions-to-a-crm)
- **HubSpot:** documents that a contact can enroll again each time the form is submitted, with actions and limits depending on subscription. That is a concrete reason to test repeat submissions rather than treat a contact record as the same thing as an inquiry. [Primary documentation](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/forms/form-automations)
- **AIBCS:** explicitly addresses current quote status, duplicate events, human exceptions, and stopping after responses or terminal statuses. Stop rules alone are not a novel differentiator. Its live page and search snippet showed different commercial details, reinforcing that competitor prices should not become market benchmarks. [Inspected service page](https://aibcs.net/automations/quote-estimate-follow-up/)
- **Forge:** explains a paid operational audit, stages, deliverables, and its own offer. Its broader price ranges are attributed to other published guides. They do not establish a representative market rate. [Inspected guide](https://forge.so/articles/ops-audit-cost)
- **KEYENCE:** provides baseline, full-cost, ROI, and payback explanations. The search topic has substantial existing treatment and overlaps Wallace Shift's current starting guide. [Inspected vendor guide](https://www.keyence.com/ss/products/software/rk/resources/blogs/how-to-calculate-rpa-roi.jsp)
- **Zapier:** identifies timeout-related replay as a possible cause of duplicate records and explains investigating run history. The business consequence can be translated into a cross-tool recovery checklist. [Primary documentation](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/8496218141453-Zap-is-creating-duplicate-data)
- **Make:** documents multiple error-handling choices; its separate incomplete-executions page states storage is disabled by default. [Error handling](https://help.make.com/Overview-of-error-handling), [incomplete executions](https://help.make.com/incomplete-executions)
- **PointWake:** already covers roofing stage rules, field ownership, duplicate events, separate lead types, and production handoffs. A proposed Wallace Shift handoff article must be narrower and supply a usable acceptance packet rather than repeat the broad pipeline guide. [Inspected guide](https://pointwake.com/blog/roofing-crm-pipeline-guide)
- **Nextriad and AI Automation Agency:** both address explicit qualification criteria and human review. Nextriad also covers missing information and evaluation. A generic “AI needs a human” article would be weak; an original evaluation exercise is needed. [Nextriad](https://nextriad.ai/compound/ai-lead-qualification/), [AI Automation Agency](https://aiautomationagency.tech/services/ai-lead-qualification/)

## Six recommended briefs

### 1. Connect website inquiries to your CRM without losing the handoff

**Proposed title:** “How to Connect Website Leads to Your CRM—and Confirm Every Inquiry Has an Owner”  
**Slug:** `connect-website-leads-to-crm`  
**Query cluster:** automate website leads to CRM; contact form CRM integration; website inquiry routing; duplicate website leads. The first cluster was directly searched in Q01; the other phrasings are proposed variants, not measured keywords.

**Buyer and outcome:** a service owner already receiving inquiries who wants a traceable intake and assignment process. Reader leaves with an implementation brief and a way to reconcile submissions with accepted records.

**Outline:** (1) distinguish submission, contact, property, and opportunity; (2) decide the receiving system and native-versus-middleware route; (3) map required fields and source information; (4) treat resubmissions, existing customers, and new jobs differently; (5) assign an owner and next action; (6) reconcile a sample from website receipt through CRM acceptance, including outages.

**Original resource:** a submission-to-owner reconciliation worksheet: submission identifier, received time, CRM contact, opportunity/job, assignment, acknowledgment, failure state, last verified action, responsible person. Include six hypothetical test cases and expected decisions, rather than screenshots suggesting a tested integration.

**Competition/gap inference:** [Autometa](https://www.autometa.in/blog/how-to-send-website-form-submissions-to-a-crm) already offers mapping/testing; [Zapier](https://zapier.com/automations/marketing/lead-management/lead-generation/create-crm-leads-from-inbound-web-form-emails-automatically) supplies a product workflow. Proposed added value is reconciliation and identity decisions for service work, not a claim that these competitors omit reliability.

**Boundary/CTA:** starts after the inquiry exists; link to the existing inquiry diagnostic and tools articles. Ends before post-sale onboarding. CTA: bring one form and receiving system to a paid automation audit, linking `/pricing#automation`.

**Evidence required:** verify the exact connector fields/actions and repeat-submission behavior against current official documentation. No assumed native integration, universal deduplication key, instant-response promise, or completed-client result.

### 3. Plan roofing estimate follow-up around status changes

**Proposed title:** “Roofing Estimate Follow-Up: Build a Workflow That Knows When to Stop”  
**Slug:** `roofing-estimate-follow-up-workflow`

**Buyer and outcome:** a roofing office with open estimates and inconsistent next actions. Reader defines which records should generate a human task, an approved reminder, or no action.

**Outline:** (1) begin with the quoting tool's existing features; (2) specify eligible estimate and revision; (3) distinguish delivered, awaiting response, replied, revised, accepted, declined, and deferred states; (4) define pauses, resumption, and accountable ownership; (5) handle repeated events and conflicting status; (6) pilot a small cohort and review actual next-action coverage.

**Original resource:** a stop/resume decision table for eight hypothetical events, including a reply after a reminder is queued and a revised quote replacing an earlier version. A separate blank follow-up record captures permission/source, current estimate, next action, owner, and review date. Do not prescribe a supposedly optimal universal cadence.

**Competition/gap inference:** [AIBCS](https://aibcs.net/automations/quote-estimate-follow-up/) already specifies stopping logic; [Zosero](https://zosero.com/solutions/roofing/roofing-estimate-follow-up-automation) and [Leadra](https://leadra.io/blog/estimate-follow-up-automation-roofing-companies/) appeared for the roofing query (snippet-only). **Boundary/CTA:** strictly pre-sale estimate decisions, not onboarding, quote calculation, storm canvassing, or insurance negotiation. CTA paid workflow audit via `/services#automation` and `/pricing#automation`.

**Evidence required:** product-specific reminder behavior needs official vendor documentation and an actual supported integration. Confirm messaging permissions, provider rules, and applicable requirements before giving implementation instructions. No claim that an inbound inquiry authorizes every automated message. Roofing examples remain hypothetical; no demo outcome becomes a client result.

### 4. Recover failed automations without repeating completed work

**Proposed title:** “What to Do When an Automation Fails: A Recovery Checklist for Small Businesses”  
**Slug:** `automation-failure-recovery-checklist`  
**Query cluster:** automation stopped working; Zapier failed workflow recovery; Make incomplete executions; automation duplicate records. Q06–Q07 tested the technical subtopics; the broader owner phrase is an editorial proposal.

**Buyer and outcome:** an owner or operations lead responsible for an existing workflow, considering managed support. Reader can capture an incident and decide who should safely resume work.

**Outline:** (1) identify affected work and limit repeated actions; (2) find the last confirmed business result; (3) distinguish invalid data, permissions, service outages, and uncertain outcomes; (4) compare retry, repair, skip, and manual completion; (5) check for duplicates or already-sent messages; (6) reconcile the backlog and assign recurrence monitoring.

**Original resource:** incident worksheet with workflow/version, time window, event identifier, last confirmed destination, already-completed side effects, retry decision, owner, recovery evidence, and prevention change. Supply a hypothetical timeout-after-CRM-write scenario for the reader to reason through.

**Competition/gap inference:** [Zapier support](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/8496218141453-Zap-is-creating-duplicate-data) and [Make documentation](https://help.make.com/Overview-of-error-handling) directly satisfy product troubleshooting. Wallace Shift should translate those mechanisms into business recovery ownership and reconciliation; do not try to outrank documentation with a diluted click-by-click article.

**Boundary/CTA:** tools article owns selection and introductory recovery. This article owns operating an incident after launch. Link to managed automation scope at `/pricing#automation`; do not imply new features are included in ongoing care.

**Evidence required:** verify current replay/retention behavior and test an isolated demonstration before adding screenshots. Avoid a universal “click retry” instruction; source records may already have changed. No claim that a provider's success status proves every business action completed.

### 5. Evaluate rules and AI using the same inquiry examples

**Proposed title:** “AI or Rules-Based Automation? Test Your Inquiry Workflow Before Choosing”  
**Slug:** `ai-vs-rules-based-automation-inquiries`  
**Query cluster:** AI vs rules based automation; AI lead qualification service business; AI inquiry triage; human review for automated lead routing. Q05 and Q11 tested the broad comparison and qualification intent.

**Buyer and outcome:** an owner with unstructured messages who is unsure whether AI adds useful interpretation. Reader defines a bounded pilot and compares it with a simpler rules baseline.

**Outline:** (1) separate explicit routing from interpreting free text; (2) identify what a rule can answer; (3) limit AI to extraction or a reviewable draft; (4) preserve unknown/conflicting information and the original inquiry; (5) compare both approaches on ordinary and ambiguous examples; (6) define review, correction, and a decision to stop or expand.

**Original resource:** a twelve-example evaluation worksheet: original inquiry, confirmed facts, expected route, unknowns, rules outcome, AI suggestion, reviewer correction, cost/time, and decision. Clearly label the examples hypothetical. Report no test accuracy until a real evaluation has been performed.

**Competition/gap inference:** [Nextriad](https://nextriad.ai/compound/ai-lead-qualification/) already covers evaluation and missing facts; [AI Automation Agency](https://aiautomationagency.tech/services/ai-lead-qualification/) includes human review. The architectural distinction can use [Anthropic's primary explanation](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents), without copying its product guidance.

**Boundary/CTA:** tools article's “Do I need AI?” FAQ remains brief. This is a single intake decision, not autonomous outbound sales, an agent roundup, or a generic AI benefits list. CTA to discuss a separately scoped AI task at `/services#automation` and `/contact`.

**Evidence required:** only relevant business criteria; no fabricated scoring accuracy, sensitive-trait inference, or silent rejection based on model confidence. Confirm current model/data-handling terms when selecting a provider. Distinguish a recommendation from an accepted action in the CRM.
