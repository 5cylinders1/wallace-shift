## Findings that change the direction

### 1. The blog and current offer are out of balance

The 16 published source files comprise **12 Business websites articles, three Workflow automation articles, and one Business systems article**. None is dedicated to CRM requirements, selection, migration, or implementation budgeting. The live services page, checked during this research, leads with custom CRMs and business systems, followed by AI integrations and connected workflows, then customer-facing websites.

That is a coverage gap relative to the business, not proof of keyword demand. It is a reason to investigate CRM buyer questions instead of commissioning more near-duplicate website comparisons. The three research tracks found relevant comparison, template, implementation, and troubleshooting results for these questions; their findings and exact query logs are linked below.

### 2. AnswerThePublic is still working from the older positioning

Read-only review of the existing Wallace Shift project found a business type of “Web Design, Web Development & Business Automation Agency.” Its summary emphasizes web design, brand credibility, rebranding, and agency services. It does not explicitly center custom CRMs. The 50 suggestions remain the original ten topic groups shown in the supplied screenshots.

**Inference:** that description likely contributes to the website-heavy recommendations. The tool's recommendation logic was not inspected, so this is not a proven causal explanation. Its “High opportunity” label is not an independent Google ranking forecast or proof that a query is asked by Wallace Shift prospects.

The table displays five numeric Volume labels—1K for professional website design, 925 for web app vs website, 173 for difference between website and web app, 117 for best website builder for service business, and 1.6K for business process automation. The other 45 say “Niche.” The country, measurement window, and methodology were not visible in that table. These are recorded tool labels, not independently verified monthly search volumes. They do not validate the new CRM topics, and “Niche” must not be interpreted as zero demand.

No project text was changed and no generation or research credits were used.

### 3. The competition is more complete than a generic checklist strategy assumes

Reviewed publishers already address workflow selection, mapping, testing, duplicates, ownership, rollback, and human review. For example, [Gangly's migration checklist](https://getgangly.com/blog/small-business-crm-migration-checklist) covers transition safeguards; [Autometa's form-to-CRM guide](https://www.autometa.in/blog/how-to-send-website-form-submissions-to-a-crm) includes mapping and testing; [Nextriad's qualification page](https://nextriad.ai/compound/ai-lead-qualification/) discusses evaluation and missing information.

Our proposed contribution is a specific, usable exercise with fictional service-business records, expected results, and evidence from a demonstration when one is performed. “We include a checklist” is not enough to establish a competitive advantage. A useful resource may be worth referencing or sharing, but this research does not predict backlinks.

### 4. Publication volume is not the strategy

Google's guidance emphasizes original, useful information, clear expertise, and meaningful value beyond summarizing other pages. It does not prescribe a word count or reward publication quantity by itself. Apply that by making each new page complete a concrete task for its reader. [Google's helpful-content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)

The same core SEO work remains relevant to Google's generative search features. A special “AI visibility” topic label is not a reason to publish another question variation without added value. [Google's generative-search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)
