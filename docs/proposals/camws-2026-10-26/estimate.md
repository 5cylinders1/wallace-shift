# Internal CAMWS effort and pricing estimate

This is a planning estimate made without Drupal exports, member spreadsheets or a tested integration. It is not time measured on the client's system. The $39,000 offer is a commercial fixed price, not an hours-times-rate invoice.

This increase does not change the offered fee. Low/high values describe favorable and adverse workload conditions; they are not statistical confidence bounds.

Stabilization hours are an estimate only; they do not cap covered defect correction. Shared meetings and quality work are not added again as a contingency line.

The 100-hour migration allowance assumes repeatable structured imports, not manually rebuilding 1,000 pages. Working allocation: 24 hours import/redirect engineering, 24 hours priority-page restructuring, 28 hours document remediation/conversion, and 24 hours validation/correction. Document complexity and actual archive inventory remain major uncertainties. Richly formatted scholarly content, Greek characters, tables, image-only files, broken historical links and attachment relationships need sampling.

The conference allowance assumes configured software and bounded adapters, with no bespoke peer-review platform. It includes interface wiring and role tests; integrated QA also appears in the separate QA row. The discovery proof must establish whether this assumption is viable.

## Economics

- Fixed implementation price: $39,000.
- Planning labor at the prior $100 estimating reference: $44,200; this is not a promised standard rate or billable amount.
- Gross return at 442 hours: approximately $88.24/hour before expenses, overhead and taxes.
- With an illustrative $2,000 of one-time expenses: approximately $83.71/hour.
- At the 576-hour adverse case with the same expenses: approximately $64.24/hour.
- One-time expense allowance is not a vendor or insurer quote; it covers development hosting/tools and other direct preparation costs as actually incurred.

At 20–25 project hours per week, 418 planned pre-stabilization hours consume roughly 17–21 productive weeks. Client approvals, holiday coverage and launch gating support the proposed 18–22 week planning window, but late inputs can extend it. Matthew confirmed this capacity alongside potential FAS work. May availability remains necessary if the launch falls then.

## Fixed price reconciliation

Phase prices: 3,900 + 4,400 + 6,000 + 8,900 + 6,400 + 4,200 + 2,800 + 2,400 = 39,000. These are deliverable prices and are not proportional hourly allocations.

Annual technology fee: 1,200 hosting/backup/monitoring + 900 software + 300 transactional mail/alias services = 2,400. Three years = 7,200.

Maintenance: 33 billable months at 600 = 19,800, with the initial three months included in implementation and no extra charge for a 90-day/calendar overlap. Routine tasks plus two requested-change hours monthly are included. Extra work requires prior written approval at 110/hour. The price presumes a stable implementation and manageable routine patch effort; it is not 24/7 staffed support.

Three-year comparison: 39,000 + 7,200 + 19,800 = 66,000. First year = 46,800; second and third years = 9,600 each. Payment processor fees and domain renewals are variable/client-direct; taxes and approved additions are excluded.

Internal sensitivity example, not a client volume forecast: 150,000 × 0.029 + 1,500 × 0.30 = 4,800/year. Three years = 14,400; added to 66,000 gives 80,400. The client-facing document instead gives the simpler $100 payment / $3.20 fee example to avoid implying a transaction forecast. Recurring Billing, currency conversion, international cards, disputes/refunds and other merchant terms may add costs.

## Risks accepted and controls

Ordinary vendor renewal increases within the offered technology capacity are Wallace Shift's risk for the quoted three-year comparison. Significant capacity expansion needs approval. Keep annual provider renewals and unused support hours explicit; do not promise that subscriptions or prepaid licenses can be refunded.

No advance deposit means discovery is financed until its $3,900 deliverable is accepted. Subsequent stage authorization is conditional on approving scope and the technical demonstration. This limits unpaid future obligations but does not eliminate execution risk.

The proposal's content limits are an offered scope qualification, not buyer-provided caps. The buyer may reject them. Do not reduce retention or accessibility commitments without written acceptance. No bid win probability or guaranteed margin is asserted.
