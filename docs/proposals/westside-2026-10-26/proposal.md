Wallace Shift builds websites and connected software that make useful information easier to find and routine work easier to manage. For WRC, that means treating readability, accessibility and the staff publishing experience as core parts of the design.

# Discovery design and WordPress development

## Begin with the tasks people need to complete

Discovery includes interviews with WRC's project owner and up to five staff or stakeholder representatives, a website and content audit, review of available analytics, and a walkthrough of findings. We will identify priority journeys such as understanding available support, applying for services, finding a service coordinator or contact, and locating an event or resource.

The audit will cover navigation, mobile use, page purpose, copy, files, existing forms and third-party services. WRC will approve a list of important consumer destinations that must be reachable within two clicks from the homepage. The navigation hierarchy will prioritize individuals and families, followed by vendors and providers, then staff information.

## Interactive design deliverables

I will provide an interactive moodboard showing typography, color, imagery and interface direction; a linked sitemap; clickable wireframes for key journeys; and **two interactive homepage design options**. WRC will select one direction for refinement. Two consolidated design review rounds are included, followed by approval of the reusable layouts.

The design will work with WRC's existing identity. A practical style guide will document color contrast, typography, headings, links, buttons, images, banners and reusable patterns so staff can maintain consistency. The proposal does not include a new organizational name or logo.

## Reusable WordPress layouts

The build includes up to ten reusable layouts: homepage; audience or service hub; standard information page; program guide; resource and document listing; news listing; news article; event listing or calendar; event detail; and contact or intake guidance. The final layout mapping will be approved during discovery.

Staff will edit structured content and approved blocks, with appropriate editor permissions, previews and revision history. Existing public search, event information, resource links and basic contact functions will be retained or replaced with accessible equivalents. Up to two simple public inquiry forms are included where needed; they will not request health records or sensitive case details.

## Integrations and ownership

Existing intake, donation, learning and service systems will be inventoried. The base proposal includes accessible links to WRC-approved external services and replacement of inaccessible embedded interfaces with an agreed accessible route. New case-management software, a private client portal, a new vendor database, payment processing and custom integrations are outside the proposed base scope.

WRC will own its content, domain, accounts and custom work created under the paid engagement. Open-source and licensed components retain their respective license terms. WRC will receive the source files, configuration notes and exportable content, with no proprietary dependency on Wallace Shift for future hosting or development.

# Accessibility testing and acceptance

## Build accessibility into the work

The agreed public website will be delivered to **WCAG 2.2 Level AA**. Accessibility review begins with the sitemap and design, continues during development and editing, and ends with an independent evaluation and retest. The project will address complete pages and journeys, including the content, forms, documents and media needed to use them.

Design and development checks will cover semantic headings, descriptive links, alternative text, contrast, keyboard operation, visible and unobscured focus, target sizes, form labels and errors, zoom and reflow, consistent navigation, and motion controls. Media will have the captions and descriptions required for its use. Automated scans will supplement manual review; an accessibility widget will not be used as evidence of conformance.

## Test with assistive technology and real tasks

Manual testing will include NVDA with a supported Windows browser, VoiceOver with Safari on Apple devices, keyboard-only use and magnification. Tests will cover every reusable layout and agreed complete journeys, with representative content and file samples and targeted review of any identified problem areas. Automated checks will run across retained HTML pages. Sampling supports evaluation and does not exempt the rest of the agreed site from the target.

I propose up to five moderated usability sessions with WRC-recruited adult participants, including people who use assistive technology where available. WRC will coordinate consent and any support needs. Participants will use public-information tasks and sample data. Optional participant compensation or specialized interpreting, if required, will be agreed separately before recruitment.

## Browser and device checks

The release candidate will be tested in current Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Opera on Windows and macOS where supported, Safari on macOS, and representative iPhone and Android devices. Testing includes responsive layouts, search, forms, navigation, files, language links and event pages. Safari testing will use supported Apple platforms.

## Evidence and release approval

WRC will receive the approved inventory, test matrix, accessibility findings and corrections, dated evaluation report, redirect checks and administrator guidance. Known failures of applicable Level A or AA criteria in the agreed deliverables must be corrected and retested before accessibility acceptance; a low severity rating will not waive a failure.

The beta presentation will allow WRC to review content and task completion before final testing. Launch requires WRC's written approval of the content, completed acceptance checks and the release plan. If a dependency prevents conformance, it must be corrected, replaced or given an agreed conforming alternative before that affected experience is published. Acceptance is a documented delivery review, not a promise that future edits or third-party changes can never introduce a new issue.

## Review and approval responsibilities

WRC's project owner will approve the content inventory, design direction, factual copy and launch. WRC will supply source files, approved brand assets and access through named accounts. WRC will also confirm permission to use photographs, stories and other supplied material, and identify records that must remain publicly available.

I will maintain the workplan, decision log and issue list, coordinate the specialists, and explain any proposed change before it affects cost or timing. Production is planned in batches so WRC can review a manageable amount of content at a time. Feedback will be consolidated into the included review rounds.

## Training and handover

Two remote administrator sessions of up to 90 minutes each are included, with a recording and concise written guide. Training will cover editing pages, creating events and news, headings and links, alternative text, document publishing, previews, permissions, and accessibility checks before release. A follow-up office hour is included after launch.

The handover includes the content and redirect registers, custom theme and plugin code, style guide, operating instructions, account ownership list and backup/restore procedure. WRC may use another provider for future work.

# Hosting maintenance and technical support

## Proposed managed environment

The proposed **$2,400 annual technology package** includes one production WordPress site and staging, up to 25 GB of production data, required site software licenses, monitoring and daily off-site backups retained for at least 30 days. Development staging is included in implementation; the annual technology term starts at public launch. Vendor selection and capacity will be confirmed during discovery.

WRC may retain compatible existing hosting instead. In that case, WRC would pay its hosting and licenses directly and the proposed technology package would be omitted by written agreement. The site will remain portable. Domain renewal, staff email services, new translation services and external subscription systems are separate from this package.

Named accounts, least-privilege permissions, multifactor authentication where supported, staging tests and a documented release process will protect administration. Backups will be monitored and a restore test performed before launch and quarterly under maintenance. Public content and synthetic examples will be used in development; the project does not include storing client health or case records in WordPress.

## Included stabilization

For **90 days after launch**, Wallace Shift will correct defects in the agreed deliverables without an hourly cap. This includes failures to meet the agreed accessibility requirements that are attributable to our delivered work. Routine updates, backup monitoring and release checks are also included during this period. New features, new content batches and changes to approved requirements are separately authorized work.

## Ongoing maintenance

The proposed **$900 monthly service** includes routine WordPress and dependency updates tested in staging, backup and monitoring checks, a monthly accessibility spot check, and up to **four hours of minor content changes or support requests**. Routine maintenance does not consume that four-hour allowance. Unused change hours expire monthly. Larger changes require a written estimate and approval at **$125 per hour**.

Maintenance begins after the 90-day stabilization period and no earlier than the start of the fourth monthly billing period after launch. No overlapping maintenance fee will be charged. Service can be ended with 30 days' notice. The quoted technology and maintenance rates are held for the first 12 months after launch; any later renewal change requires notice and agreement.

## Response and escalation

Support hours are Monday through Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Central, excluding U.S. federal holidays. A site outage, suspected compromise or blocking failure in a key public journey receives an initial response within four business hours. Routine requests receive an initial response within two business days. These are response targets, not guaranteed resolution times. This proposal does not include a staffed 24-hour help desk.

WRC will receive a monthly summary of work, requests, checks and remaining change hours. A full independent annual accessibility reassessment is recommended and can be separately quoted; the monthly spot check is not a repeat of the launch evaluation.
